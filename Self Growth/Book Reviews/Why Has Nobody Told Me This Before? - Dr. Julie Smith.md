# "Why Has Nobody Told Me This Before?" explained like you're five: a therapist wrote down the tools she gives her patients and most of them are things you should have learned in school

![r/explainlikeimfivebook - Why Ups Has suot Nobody Told Me This Before? W}ILIESMMT](https://preview.redd.it/why-has-nobody-told-me-this-before-explained-like-youre-v0-gkh0r6mgzmjh1.jpeg?auto=webp&s=b64f134c7ef2089600fe51731c847265011a2c85)

Dr. Julie Smith was a clinical psychologist who started posting therapy concepts on social media and quickly realized something. Millions of people were encountering basic mental health tools for the first time through her videos. Not complex techniques. Foundational ones. The kind of stuff that should be taught alongside algebra and history but never is.

**Mood Follows Action, Not the Other Way Around**

- Most people wait until they feel motivated to do something. Smith says that's backwards. Action comes first. Motivation shows up after you start. You don't feel like going for a walk, but you go, and ten minutes in your mood has shifted. The trap is believing you need the feeling before you can take the step. You don't. You need the step before the feeling arrives.
    

**Thoughts Are Not Facts**

- Your brain generates thoughts constantly and most of them are noise. "Nobody likes me." "I'm going to fail." "Something bad is going to happen." Smith says the skill isn't stopping these thoughts. It's learning to observe them without obeying them. A thought can pass through your mind without becoming a decision, a belief, or a spiral. You don't have to argue with every sentence your brain produces. You can just let it keep talking and not follow it out the door.
    

**Name It to Tame It**

- The simple act of labeling an emotion reduces its intensity. Brain imaging shows that when you say "I'm feeling anxious" the amygdala's activity decreases. The emotion doesn't disappear. But naming it moves processing from the emotional brain to the rational brain, giving you just enough space to choose a response instead of being hijacked by a reaction.
    

**Where it overstates things:**

The book reads more like a toolkit than a deep exploration. If you've done therapy or read other psychology books, some of this will feel familiar. It's wide but not always deep.

But that's also the point. Not everyone has access to a therapist. This book is the next best thing for millions of people who need the basics and were never given them.
