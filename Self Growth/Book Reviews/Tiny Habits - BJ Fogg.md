# Tiny Habits by BJ Fogg taught me that every habit I failed to build failed for the same reason and it had nothing to do with motivation.

![r/selfimprovement_books - Tiny Habits by BJ Fogg taught me that every habit I failed to build failed for the same reason and it had nothing to do with motivation.](https://preview.redd.it/tiny-habits-by-bj-fogg-taught-me-that-every-habit-i-failed-v0-ah0tik42rxih1.jpeg?auto=webp&s=e5540e2e6871087dc2ce4a3248c74eca9e1fa02d)

BJ Fogg runs the Behavior Design Lab at Stanford. He's spent 20 years studying why people do what they do. His conclusion: motivation is the worst foundation for behavior change. It fluctuates daily. It vanishes under stress. And every habit system built on motivation collapses the moment you have a bad week.

His alternative is embarrassingly small. So small that your brain can't talk you out of it.

Want to start flossing? Don't commit to flossing all your teeth. Commit to flossing one tooth. Want to start exercising? Don't commit to an hour at the gym. Commit to two pushups. Want to start reading? Don't commit to a chapter. Commit to opening the book.

That's it. That's the system. And the reason it works is neurological, not motivational.

**Behavior happens when three things converge: motivation, ability, and a prompt.** Fogg calls this the Behavior Model. If any of the three is missing, the behavior doesn't happen. Most people try to fix behavior change by increasing motivation. Fogg says that's the hardest variable to control. The easier fix is to make the ability requirement so low that even your worst day can't stop you. Two pushups require almost zero motivation. But they still create the neural pathway. And once the pathway exists, scaling up happens naturally.

**Anchor the new habit to something you already do.** Fogg calls this the "anchor moment." After I pour my coffee, I do two pushups. After I sit at my desk, I open my journal. After I put my phone on the charger at night, I read one page. The existing behavior becomes the trigger for the new one. No alarm needed. No reminder app. No willpower. The anchor does the work because it's already automated.

**Celebrate immediately.** This is the part most people skip and it's the part that makes the whole system stick. Fogg says you need to create a small positive emotion immediately after performing the habit. A fist pump. A quiet "nice." A genuine moment of self-acknowledgment. It sounds silly. The neuroscience isn't silly. The celebration releases a small dopamine hit that wires the behavior as something your brain wants to repeat. You're not rewarding yourself with something external. You're training your brain to associate the behavior with a positive signal.

I built three habits using this method that had defeated me for years. Journaling, stretching, and reading before bed. All three failed multiple times using willpower-based approaches. All three stuck within two weeks using Fogg's method. Not because I became more disciplined. Because the habits were so small my brain never registered them as threats worth resisting.

Fogg says something in the book that reframed my entire relationship with change: "People change best by feeling good, not by feeling bad." Guilt, shame, and self-criticism don't build habits. They destroy them. Tiny success, repeated daily, is the only thing that compounds.
