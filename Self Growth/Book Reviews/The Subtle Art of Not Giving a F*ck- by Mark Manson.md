# This book told me to stop being positive and it was the best advice I ever got

![r/Growthmindsetbookclub - This book told me to stop being positive and it was the best advice I ever got](https://preview.redd.it/this-book-told-me-to-stop-being-positive-and-it-was-the-v0-3m8hq97q4ckh1.png?width=640&crop=smart&auto=webp&s=6479ec59c9aefaa1ff5484d4cb618961196d7d1f)

Read "The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck" by Mark Manson expecting another feel-good self-help book. Instead got a philosophy that made me question everything I believed about happiness and success.

You're going to have problems no matter what. Choose better problems.

Manson's core argument: life is essentially an endless series of problems. Solving one just reveals the next. Happy people don't have fewer problems. They have problems they enjoy solving. Getting fit is a problem. Being out of shape is a problem. You don't escape problems. You trade up for ones that are worth having.

The desire for a pain-free life is causing most of your pain.

Most suffering comes from the expectation that you shouldn't be suffering. You feel bad, then feel bad about feeling bad. Manson argues that accepting pain as a built-in feature, not a bug, removes this second layer of suffering. Struggle isn't evidence that something is wrong. It's evidence that you're alive and attempting things that matter.

You're not special. And that's actually freeing.

The self-esteem movement taught everyone they're exceptional. Manson points out the math problem: everyone can't be above average. Believing you're special creates pressure to achieve extraordinary things. Accepting that you're ordinary removes that pressure. Ordinary people do meaningful things constantly. You don't need to be exceptional to matter.

What pain are you willing to sustain?

Everyone wants the reward. Few want the process. Manson reframes the question: don't ask what you want. Ask what struggle you're willing to tolerate. You want the fit body but not the early mornings. You want the successful business but not the rejection. Your willingness to suffer for something determines whether you'll actually get it.

Values determine the quality of your problems.

Bad values create unsolvable problems. Chasing popularity means your self-worth depends on others. Chasing wealth means there's always someone richer. Manson argues that good values are internal, controllable, and evidence-based. Honesty, curiosity, humility. These create problems you can actually solve through your own actions.

Certainty is the enemy of growth.

The more certain you are about something, the less open you are to new information. Manson advocates for holding beliefs loosely. Being wrong isn't failure. It's data. People who need to be right all the time stop learning because learning requires admitting you didn't know something.

Action isn't the result of motivation. Motivation is the result of action.

Most people wait to feel motivated before acting. Manson inverts this. Start moving first. Motivation follows action, not the other way around. If you wait until you feel like going to the gym, you'll rarely go. If you just go, the motivation shows up mid-workout.

"The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck" gave me permission to stop pretending everything needs to be positive and start dealing with life as it actually is.

What problem have you been avoiding that might actually be worth solving?
